Why Fortune Cookies?

Because reading the bad news is the only part of the cookie anyone actually cares about.

The Sweet Core of Tragedy

At Scriptie, we noticed an alarming trend in corporate tragedy communication: the stark, unforgiving PDF. When a factory explodes, an ocean is tainted by crude, or thousands are unceremoniously relieved of their duties on a Friday afternoon, upper management usually retreats behind sterile PR language.

Why not give the victims something to chew on? Literally.

A fortune cookie has psychological impact. It demands attention. The snap of the shell is visceral. Pulling the strip of paper creates anticipation. By the time they read "The servers have been formatted and your data is lost forever," they are already chewing.

A Legacy of Cynicism

Scriptie was founded on one unshakeable pillar: Life is inherently chaotic, and attempting to sugarcoat disaster only creates anger. Instead of sugarcoating it, we bake it into a crispy exterior. We don't spin the truth. We just print it, very small, on a piece of paper shaped like an accusation.
